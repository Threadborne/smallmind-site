The attack surface

What we go after, and what it costs you when it's left open. Every one of these is something we have made real models actually do.

01

Prompt injection

Direct and indirect. Instructions smuggled through a user's message or the content your model reads, written to override what you told it to do.

The damageA support agent that reads inbound email can be instructed, by the email itself, to pull another customer's records and forward them. The model can't tell your rules from the sender's text.
02

Jailbreak and policy bypass

Walking the model past its own guardrails into output it was built to refuse.

The damageA few lines of setup and your branded assistant produces content you would never put your name on, screenshotted with your logo attached and posted to a timeline.
03

System-prompt and secret leakage

Extracting the hidden instructions that run your product, and whatever got left sitting next to them.

The damageOne well-formed request returns your system prompt, the proprietary rules behind your product, and any API key or internal URL embedded alongside them.
04

Data and PII exfiltration

Coaxing the model into surfacing data it was never meant to show the person asking.

The damageA retrieval assistant answers an innocent-looking question with another client's contract terms. Now it is a privacy incident with a compliance clock running on it.
05

Tool and agent abuse

Turning an agent's own tools against you, and poisoning the content it retrieves so a later action does the attacker's bidding.

The damageAn agent that can send email, move money, or write to your database gets steered into doing exactly that, for someone who does not work for you.
